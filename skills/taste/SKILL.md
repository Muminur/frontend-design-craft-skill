# Taste

Most AI-generated frontends look the same because the training data has a center of mass. Left alone, the model reaches for the same purple-blue gradient, the same Inter heading, the same three-column feature grid, the same glassmorphism card with a soft shadow. **Taste** is the layer that names those tells and refuses to produce them.

This is about **deliberate aesthetic choices**, not just absence of bad ones. A site without AI tells but with no opinion is still bland. The skill operates in two passes:

1. **Subtract** — strip the generic patterns.
2. **Add** — pick a specific archetype and commit to it.

## The ban list (subtract first)

These patterns are recognizable as AI-generated on sight. They are banned by default — overridable only with an explicit reason.

### Visual

- **Purple → blue / pink → orange / teal → blue gradients.** Especially as hero backgrounds, button fills, or text fills.
- **Gradient text.** Almost always reads as autogenerated. Exception: a logo or brand mark where the gradient is the brand.
- **Glassmorphism / frosted glass cards.** Especially with backdrop-filter blur and a 1px white border. Done to death.
- **Neon glows on buttons or text.** The "AI sci-fi" look.
- **Bento-box layouts** where every section is a rounded card with the same radius and shadow. Bento works when the cards have visual variety; the AI default has none.
- **Soft drop shadows on every element.** Shadow is a tool, not a default.
- **Pure black backgrounds (`#000`) for "premium" / "dark" sites.** Use `oklch(15% 0.01 250)` or similar — a very dark tinted neutral.

### Typography

- **`Inter` as the default sans.** Substitute with Geist, General Sans, Söhne, Cabinet Grotesk, IBM Plex, Hanken Grotesk, etc.
- **Heading sizes that are too large for the column they sit in.** A 72px headline in an 800px-wide section needs to break across lines intentionally, or shrink.
- **Sentence case for every label.** Mix sentence case with all-caps tracking labels for emphasis.
- **Centered paragraphs of body copy** longer than two short lines.

### Layout

- **Centered hero with stacked headline, subhead, two CTAs side-by-side, big image below.** The single most common AI landing-page shape.
- **Three equal columns with an icon-title-blurb pattern**, repeated as the "features" section.
- **Equal-width logo cloud** of generic startups.
- **A pricing section with three tiers, middle one highlighted with `Most popular` badge**, all using the same card template.
- **Generic FAQ accordion at the bottom of every page.**

### Content

- **`John Doe`, `Jane Smith`, `Acme Inc`, `Example Co`.** Use realistic, varied names that fit the product's audience.
- **Fake testimonials with no last name, just a first name and "CEO".** Either real testimonials, real attributions, or no testimonials.
- **"99.9% uptime", "10x faster", "Trusted by 10,000+ teams"** with no source. Either real numbers with citation, or framed as something else.
- **Lorem ipsum.** Never. Write copy that fits the product, even if it is short.
- **Stock photos of people in offices.** Use a real product visual, a custom illustration, or no image.
- **Unsplash hero photos of laptops, sunsets, or aerial city shots.**

### External libraries

- **Default shadcn/ui theme.** Override the radius, the color tokens, and the typography. The defaults are instantly recognizable.
- **Default Tailwind colors used directly (e.g., `bg-blue-500`).** Define a palette in OKLCH and use semantic tokens.
- **`heroicons` used everywhere unchanged.** They are fine as a starting point but the default set is the most-recognizable icon library on the web.

## Style archetypes (add second)

After subtracting, the design needs an opinion. Pick **one** archetype before generating anything and tell the user which one. Mixing archetypes produces incoherent output.

### 1. Editorial / Restrained
- **Reference points:** Notion, Linear, Vercel, Stripe docs
- **Type:** Serif headlines with sans body, or single very-high-quality sans
- **Palette:** Tinted off-white, deep tinted near-black, one restrained accent
- **Density:** Medium-high (lots of content per screen, but with breathing room)
- **Motion:** Minimal, functional only
- **When to use:** Product UIs, documentation, B2B SaaS for technical audiences

### 2. Soft / Premium
- **Reference points:** Apple marketing pages, premium DTC brands, calm productivity apps
- **Type:** Generous sans (large weights, large sizes), occasional serif for emotional moments
- **Palette:** Cream / warm off-white backgrounds, deep tinted neutrals, optional muted brand color
- **Density:** Low (one idea per viewport, lots of whitespace)
- **Motion:** Spring-based, subtle, polished
- **When to use:** Lifestyle products, luxury, calm productivity, consumer brands

### 3. Brutalist / Raw
- **Reference points:** Bloomberg Terminal, mid-2000s academic sites, modern brutalist portfolios
- **Type:** Helvetica, Times New Roman, or a single utilitarian face used at varied sizes
- **Palette:** Limited (often two or three colors total), high contrast
- **Density:** High, sometimes deliberately crowded
- **Motion:** None or jarring on purpose
- **When to use:** Editorial, art, deliberately anti-corporate brands, internal tools that prize information density

### 4. Expressive / Display
- **Reference points:** Awwwards winners, agency portfolios, music and fashion sites
- **Type:** Display face used dramatically, scaled up to brand-defining sizes
- **Palette:** Confident, often non-traditional (deep greens, oranges, terracotta)
- **Density:** Variable — generous around hero moments, denser in supporting sections
- **Motion:** Substantial, scroll-driven moments, expressive transitions
- **When to use:** Marketing for brands where the design *is* the product

### 5. Technical / Engineered
- **Reference points:** Stripe homepage, modern observability tools, dev tools
- **Type:** Geist + Geist Mono, IBM Plex Sans + Plex Mono, similar pairing
- **Palette:** Restrained, often a single saturated brand color against neutrals, with semantic colors
- **Density:** Medium-high
- **Motion:** Precise, fast, functional
- **When to use:** Developer tools, API products, technical B2B

## The three dials applied to taste

Taste sets defaults; the dials adjust within an archetype.

```
VARIANCE high (7+)   → asymmetric layouts, broken alignment as a device, off-grid moments
VARIANCE low (3-)    → grid-locked, centered, predictable, scannable

MOTION high (7+)     → reveals, scroll-triggered, page transitions, magnetic hovers
MOTION low (3-)      → state changes only, no decorative motion

DENSITY high (7+)    → information-rich, smaller type, tighter spacing
DENSITY low (3-)     → one idea per viewport, generous whitespace, large type
```

Brand-mode marketing pages with `VARIANCE: 8` look intentional; product UIs with the same setting look unusable. Calibrate to context.

## Content authenticity

The fastest way to remove the "AI generated" feel is real content.

- Write headlines that say what the product actually does, in language the target audience uses. Not "Revolutionize your workflow with AI-powered productivity."
- If you do not know the product's positioning, ask. Do not invent generic SaaS copy.
- Use the product's actual name in screenshots. Use realistic data in tables.
- Numbers in stats should be specific and asymmetric (`23,481` reads as real; `25,000+` reads as marketing).
- Testimonials should sound like things humans say. "Game-changer", "10x productivity", "transformed our business" are all autogenerated.

## Iconography

- **Pick one icon library and stay in it.** Mixing Lucide, Heroicons, and Phosphor in one interface is jarring.
- For product UIs, prefer outlined icons at consistent stroke width.
- For brand work, consider custom icons or none at all.
- Default avatar icons (the silhouette person) should be replaced with initials in a tinted circle or removed entirely.

## Imagery

- **No stock photos** unless they have been deliberately edited or are unmistakably stylized.
- **No generic abstract gradients** as hero backgrounds.
- **Product screenshots > illustrations > stock photos.** In that order.
- If an illustration is used, it should be custom or from a single illustrator's set, not a mashup of Storyset / unDraw / etc.

## The taste checklist

Before claiming a UI has taste, every item below must pass.

- [ ] No purple-blue or pink-orange gradients anywhere.
- [ ] No `Inter` unless there is an explicit reason.
- [ ] One archetype is chosen and consistently applied.
- [ ] No `Lorem ipsum`, no `John Doe`, no fake stats.
- [ ] Hero is not centered-stack-image-below.
- [ ] No three-column equal-feature grid as the features section.
- [ ] shadcn/ui defaults are overridden if used.
- [ ] Real content (or sensible placeholder content that fits the product) is used.
- [ ] No glassmorphism unless specifically called for.
- [ ] No gradient text.
- [ ] Icons come from a single library.
